# Analyzing-Cardiovascular-Health-Outcomes-Across-American-Communities-Using-X

Abstract: 
Objectives: Periodic public health surveillance of cardiovascular health (CVH) is essential for understanding the health of U.S. communities. This study evaluates the feasibility of using Twitter/X data to assess CVH behaviors, focusing on four Life’s Essential 8™ (LE8) behaviors—Eat Better, Be More Active, Quit Tobacco, Get Healthy Sleep—and two additional behaviors (Quit Alcohol and Quit Substance Use), and examines associations with community-level outcomes.

Setting and Design: 
We analyzed 32 million U.S.-based Twitter/X posts (2017–2022) containing CVH-related keywords and linked county-level tweet-derived signals to health outcomes from PLACES and USDA datasets across U.S. communities defined by American Communities Project (ACP). Analyses included multilevel modeling of tweet-derived behaviors and CVH outcomes, topic modeling of community-specific CVH discourse, predictive modeling across ACP communities, and tests of community-level differences.
 
Primary and Secondary Outcome measures: Primary outcomes were county-level CVH measures from PLACES and USDA datasets; secondary outcomes included topic patterns in CVH-related tweets.

Results: Multilevel models showed significant associations for Be More Active (β = −5.59, p < 0.001), Get Healthy Sleep (β = 9.72, p < 0.001), and Quit Alcohol (β = 1.96, p = 0.006). Significant topics were identified in 6 of 14 ACP communities, primarily related to smoking and alcohol. Predictive performance was strong for Be More Active (r = 0.84) and Quit Tobacco (r = 0.76), and moderate for Get Healthy Sleep (r = 0.58) (all p < 0.001). Tweet distributions differed across communities (p < 0.05).
Conclusions: Social media data like Twitter/X provide an informative supplement for monitoring CVH across U.S. communities. Tweet-derived signals were associated with survey-based measures and showed stronger predictive performance for Be More Active, Quit Tobacco, and Get Healthy Sleep, highlighting the importance of behavior- and context-specific evaluation for population health monitoring.
